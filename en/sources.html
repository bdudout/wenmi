Sources and credits

Numbers match the note calls in both articles. Web pages were consulted between 4 and 10 October 2026.

Daniel Bourdeau located these telegrams in the Japanese archives, published their transcriptions, broke the five-vowel key family of Sun Yat-sen's circle and flagged 文密 as an open problem. This investigation directly extends his work [1, 2], published under CC BY 4.0.

Prior work

  1. Daniel Bourdeau, "Sun Yat-sen's circle on the wire, 1916–17", published 18 September 2026, updated 24 September 2026. CC BY 4.0.
  2. Daniel Bourdeau, repository dbourdeau/cyphersolver (transcriptions, notes, scripts famtest.py, hankow.py).

Primary sources (Japanese archives, JACAR)

  1. JACAR, Diplomatic Archives of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Japan, series 1.6.1.4-2-1-2 "特定人ノ発受郵便物取調", vol. 4: B03050089900 (images 0329–0387: telegrams 0348 of 23 June and 0386 of 30 June 1916), B03050090000 (images 0388–0446: 0406 of 12 July, 0415 of 18 July, 0416 of 19 July 1916; for the second channel, 0413 of 17 July and 0416a) and B03050090100 (images 0447–0501: 0455 of 13 September, 0462 of 30 September 1916; for the second channel, 0449, 0455a, 0467, 0473, 0489, 0494, 0496 and 0498, September to November 1916).
  2. JACAR B03050090200, same series, vol. 4: telegrams of March and June 1917, including 0509 and 0511 (Sun Yat-sen ↔ Dai Jitao, the 1917 文密, not broken).
  3. JACAR B03050730500: report 政機密第26号 from the consul-general in Hankou, 瀬川淺之進, to Minister 石井菊次郎, 9 May 1916 ("−111" method).
  4. JACAR B03050738800: report 政機第28号, same author, 11 May 1916 ("reverse the four digits, then −111" method), with copies of the April 1916 telegrams.
