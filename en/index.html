文密

The Wenmi code, broken

In the summer of 1916, a Chinese envoy in Tokyo, Zhao Shen, was negotiating Japanese rifles, aircraft and loans for the rebel provinces of southern China. His telegrams, enciphered under the name 文密 (Wenmi), were intercepted and copied by the Japanese administration, then lay for more than a century in Tokyo's diplomatic archives. Both channels he used in 1916 can now be read, for the most part.

An investigation by Brice Dudout, carried out with Claude (Anthropic), building on Daniel Bourdeau's work on the telegrams of Sun Yat-sen's circle.

Where things stand

Both 1916 channels are broken in principle: in each, a regular grid turns each half of a telegraph code into a syllable.

  • Tokyo → Zhaoqing (June–September, 7 telegrams, G0 grid): text settled for the most part, about twenty uncertain characters, marked.
  • Tokyo ↔ Yunnan (July–November, 15 forms, Y0 grid, broken on 11 October 2026): text fully re-read by a human, though many characters remain doubtful.
  • The 1917 messages bearing the same name (Sun Yat-sen ↔ Dai Jitao) are not broken: they use a different alphabet.
